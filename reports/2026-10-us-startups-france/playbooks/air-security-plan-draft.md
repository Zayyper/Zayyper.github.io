## AIR Security France launch plan at a glance

**Verdict: conditional go, kept small.** Condition, by 29 January 2027: AIR signs a non-exclusive referral agreement (apporteur d'affaires) paying at least 10% of first-year contract value, with deal registration, and gives written dates for SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 and EU hosting. Otherwise the operator stops, having spent about €300.

AIR has $50M, a plan to sell in Europe [1] and a strong proof point in Plugin4Shell [5]. But 18 searches up to 7 October 2026 found no partner programme, European job posting or EMEA hire; the company is six months old, with about 40 staff in Israel [3]. French banks will not quickly approve an uncertified vendor without EU hosting under DORA [20], and security sales already take 7 to 14 months [22]. In 2027 the operator is a door-opener paid on success, aimed at pharma and scale-ups, while bank deals are started for 2028. Probability-weighted 2027 revenue (€1,730) roughly equals expected cost (€1,800).

## What you would sell and to whom

AIR sells an inline "firewall for AI agents". It finds agents across endpoints, cloud and SaaS, vets every skill, plug-in and MCP server, revokes bad components everywhere, and filters what enters an agent's context [1][2]. Plugin4Shell, disclosed on 18 September 2026, was a zero-click remote-code-execution flaw in Claude Code, Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini CLI [5]. AIR's pricing, deployment model and certifications are not public.

**Buyer:** the CISO (RSSI) of a large regulated group, with the security architect who owns developer tools or the AI platform. **Trigger:** coding agents running unvetted add-ons; ANSSI and Germany's BSI have issued guidance on AI coding assistants [27]. **Blockers:** supplier-risk review, the data protection officer, the DORA register owner.

**Sales cycle:** 7 to 14 months for enterprise cybersecurity, each deal including a SOC 2 review [22]; 6 to 9 months in finance, 9 to 18 in regulated sectors [23]. Estimate for France: 4 to 7 months at a scale-up, 6 to 10 in pharma, 12 to 18 at a bank.

## The deal with AIR

| Option | How it works | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial agent (Code de commerce L134-1) | Standing mandate to negotiate for AIR; RSAC registration required [7]. The L134-12 end-of-contract payment cannot be waived and is about two years of gross commission in case law [6][7]; EU case law (Ingmar, 2000) probably applies it even under New York law (verify). | US lawyers will refuse; under 10% |
| Reseller | Buys licences, carries contract risk, needs banks' supplier approval | Impossible for a micro-entreprise |
| Referral partner | Paid on closed deals. CyberArk pays 5 to 10% of contract value [8]; Hexagon 15% of first-year SaaS value [9]; payment often 60 days after the quarter the vendor is paid [10]. | **Realistic route** |
| Contracted France lead | If AIR wants France staffed, it will want a full-time employee, probably via an employer-of-record (estimate), not a 20-hour contractor | Employee route only; decide in advance |

**What to ask for:**
1. 15% of first-year value, paid pilots included, plus 5% on year-2 renewals.
2. Deal registration protecting each account for 12 months, binding even after AIR hires someone for EMEA.
3. Payment 30 days after AIR is paid.
4. A named sales engineer (Israel is one hour ahead of Paris).
5. Written dates for SOC 2 and ISO 27001; a GDPR data processing agreement; the hosting region; an on-premise option.
6. A €2,500-a-month retainer for three months, in exchange for a French NIS2 and DORA control map and 10 qualified meetings.

**AIR's likely answer:** an informal yes to about 10% "once you bring a meeting"; no to exclusivity and the retainer; "in progress" on certifications. Why 30%: funding is earmarked partly for European sales [1] and referrals cost nothing until a sale, but a 40-person, US-focused Israeli team [3] usually ignores unsolicited partners (estimate).

**Fallback:** test the account map for one month with Zenity, Noma or France's Giskard, or introduce AIR to a bank-approved French integrator that holds the bank contract.

## Why this can fail

- **Silence.** The likeliest outcome: no reply from a six-month-old, US-focused team [3].
- **The certification wall.** Under DORA, banks must assess a supplier before signing and must secure audit rights for critical functions [20][21]. AIR has no SOC 2, ISO 27001 or EU hosting, so bank revenue moves to H2 2028 at best.
- **An EMEA hire takes the accounts.** Unlike a commercial agent, a referral partner has no legal right to a payment when the relationship ends [6].
- **Free pilots, slow payment.** Free pilots earn nothing; commission arrives 2 to 5 months after signature (estimate from [10]).
- **Bundling.** Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, Check Point and SentinelOne already offer agent discovery or MCP controls (dossier); a consolidating CISO sees AIR as a feature.
- **Acquisition.** Four AI guardrail vendors were bought in two months in 2025 [26]. A buyer would cancel referral agreements.
- **NIS2 is not a 2027 budget line.** The bill is scheduled in the National Assembly from 7 October 2026 [18], with no outcome reported yet, after the EU took France to court [19]; decrees and ANSSI designations follow, so deadlines come later (estimate).
- **Credibility.** The operator has no track record here, and CISOs are flooded with AI-security pitches (estimate).

## Kill criteria

- **13 Nov 2026:** no call with an AIR executive after three contacts and a warm-introduction attempt. Stop.
- **29 Jan 2027:** no signed agreement at 10% or more with deal registration, or no written certification dates. Kill.
- **31 Mar 2027:** fewer than 6 first meetings with CISOs or architects. Cut to under 3 h/week.
- **30 Apr 2027:** fewer than 3 opportunities accepted by AIR. Kill.
- **15 Jul 2027:** no pilot running, or no audit date from AIR. Drop the banks.
- **10 Dec 2027:** no signed order or paid pilot. Stop investing; keep commission rights on registered accounts.
- **Any date:** AIR is acquired, or its EMEA hire ignores registered deals. Negotiate a final payment; exit.
