## Ortet at a glance

Ortet is a New York AI lab that launched on 29 September 2026 to build one patient-centred foundation model for health [1][2]. It starts with a $500 million commitment from Thoreau, an Apollo-backed US healthcare platform, and has no disclosed European presence [1][12]. For France, it is a new, well-funded rival for health-AI talent and data, and a possible tenant for Paris research sites.

## Product and business model

Ortet says it will build one patient-centred foundation model plus its own compute, data and deployment stack [1]. The model connects "biological, clinical, operational, and financial dimensions of health" to "learn from every patient, for every patient" [1][10]. The company calls this a "shared intelligence foundation" for discovery, clinical care, operations and financial administration [1].

**Company statements.** The $500M funds compute and data infrastructure, model training and deployment, and research and talent [1]. Ortet says it has secured its initial GPU cluster [1]. Cho said Ortet "is building at every layer of the AI stack" so research becomes systems that "understand the complete context of a patient" [5].

**Press claims.** HIT Consultant contrasts Ortet with narrow tools such as ambient scribing or claims scrubbing [5]. AllSci reports spending on GPU clusters, proprietary data pipelines and engineers [9]. HIT Consultant says Ortet will deploy alongside provider, payer and life-sciences partners (single source) [5].

**Who pays.** Not disclosed; Ortet was pre-revenue at launch (data file). Likely buyers are health systems, payers and life-sciences firms. Pricing, licensing and open or closed weights are not disclosed.

**Data strategy.** No data source or data agreement is named. Thoreau's partner Ensemble Health Partners runs revenue-cycle work for more than 200 hospitals and $55 billion of net patient revenue [14][19]. An Ortet–Ensemble data link is not documented; treat it as an inference.

**Compute and hosting.** Cluster provider, size and location are not disclosed. Henri Dwyer built Genentech's GPU cluster, described as the largest in life sciences [3]. No EU hosting is disclosed.

## Regulatory fit for France and the EU

EU health-data law is a headwind for Ortet now and a partial tailwind after 2029.

- **GDPR and CNIL.** Health data is specially protected; some uses need a CNIL declaration or authorisation [36]. CNIL issued a practical guide on health AI on 5 March 2026, after a HAS–CNIL guide on 16 February 2026 [38]. Its 2026 programme updates reference methodologies so more research runs on a declaration [37].
- **EU AI Act.** The Council approved the Digital Omnibus on 29 June 2026 [34]. High-risk AI in products such as medical devices applies from 2 August 2028; Annex III systems from 2 December 2027 [34][35]. General-purpose model obligations kept their timeline [35].
- **Medical-device rules.** A model used for diagnosis or treatment could fall under device rules and the Annex I regime [34][35]. Each clinical use needs its own conformity route.
- **EHDS.** Most secondary-use rules apply from 2029; clinical-trial and genetic data from 2031 [31]. Access bodies issue permits, and algorithm training is an allowed purpose [31].
- **Health Data Hub.** The Hub is leaving Microsoft Azure for a SecNumCloud cloud by end 2026, citing the US CLOUD Act [32]. Scaleway won a €180M contract on 23 April 2026 (single source) and holds HDS certification [33].

**What Ortet would need.** An EU entity and data protection officer. HDS-certified, EU-controlled hosting and a CNIL route per dataset. AI Act files for any general-purpose model, device conformity for clinical uses, and an EHDS permit plan.
