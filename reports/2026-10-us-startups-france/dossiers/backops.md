## Product and business model

BackOps calls itself a "resolution layer" that executes approved back-office work across systems, internal teams and outside parties [1]. In March 2026 it used the broader label "AI-native operating system" [6].

- **Relay** (flagship) works across email, Slack, messaging and service tickets. It files carrier claims, starts reshipments and answers customer inquiries [6][7].
- Relay ingests a customer's standard operating procedures and deploys agents across order management, claims, shipping, billing and customer service [2][3].
- **AI Process Center** records how employees complete workflows and turns them into automated actions [7].
- Fulfill.com's partner page claims integrations with 100+ WMS and other systems (single source) [15].

Buyers are large enterprises in grocery, retail, logistics and distribution [2]. Series A coverage named automakers, global retailers, grocery chains and US machinery suppliers [7].

Pricing and contract basis are not disclosed. Sales job posts in Los Angeles and Chicago point to direct enterprise selling [16]. The company's claimed edge is agents that act on documented procedures rather than only alert. No SOC 2, ISO 27001, hosting region or EU data residency was found. AI model providers are not disclosed.

## Regulatory fit for France and the EU

Regulation is mixed: e-invoicing creates demand, while carrier law, GDPR and the AI Act add work.

- **French e-invoicing.** Since 1 September 2026, all French VAT-registered firms must receive e-invoices; large firms and ETI must also issue them [25][26]. SMEs issue from 1 September 2027, through a Plateforme Agréée [25]. Tailwind: billing disputes move into structured data. BackOps would need connectors to approved platforms.
- **Carrier liability.** CMR caps road-carrier liability at 8.33 SDR per kilogram of gross weight [29]. Claims expire after one year, or three with intent [29]. Headwind: US claims logic must be rebuilt for CMR and French domestic rules.
- **EU AI Act.** A provisional deal of 7 May 2026 delays Annex III high-risk duties to 2 December 2027 [27]. Parliament voted on 16 June 2026; Council approval is pending [28]. Claims and billing automation does not appear to be a listed high-risk use. Low headwind.
- **GDPR.** Claims files hold consignee names, addresses and photos. BackOps would need a data processing agreement, a subprocessor list, a transfer mechanism and ideally EU hosting.
- **CSRD.** Omnibus I, approved 24 February 2026, limits reporting to firms above 1,000 employees and €450M turnover [30]. Large French logistics groups likely stay in scope. BackOps does no emissions reporting, so the tailwind is indirect.
- **Customs.** No customs feature is documented; EU customs exceptions would be a new process.
