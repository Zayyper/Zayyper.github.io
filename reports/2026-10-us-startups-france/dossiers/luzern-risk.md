## Luzern Risk at a glance

Luzern Risk is a New York captive insurance manager that uses software and AI to form and run captives for mid-market firms. It raised a $45M Series B led by Insight Partners on 17 September 2026, its third round since 2023 [1][3][8]. For France, it arrives as French reinsurance captives grow from 4 (2019) to 22 to 24 (late 2025) under the 2023 tax reform [30][35].

## Product and business model

Luzern sells end-to-end captive management: it designs a captive, forms it, coordinates specialists and then manages it once funded [5]. Its directory listing names nine services: management, reinsurance and fronting procurement, legal, tax, audit, accounting, actuarial, feasibility, and regulatory governance [5].

- **How it works (company statement).** CEO Gabriel Weiss says the firm splits captive work into "nine distinct work streams" and acts as "air traffic control" [15].
- **The AI part (company statement).** The Series A release cited a modern interface, "agentic AI", full API compatibility and automations [12][14]. The Series B will fund AI capabilities and more automated captive administration [1][8].
- **Who buys.** Mid-market companies up to public companies, often through brokers. Luzern works alongside brokers, fronting carriers, reinsurers and advisers, and runs a broker partnership page [9][21].
- **Domiciles.** United States, Cayman Islands, Bermuda and "Europe"; no European domicile is named [5].
- **Fees and pricing.** Not disclosed.
- **Licences, certifications, hosting.** Not disclosed. No search result named a captive-manager licence, SOC 2 report or cloud host.
- **Differentiation.** Luzern targets companies below the Fortune 500. Weiss says captive use there drops off sharply because those firms lack large risk departments [15].

## Regulatory fit for France and the EU

France's reform is a tailwind for demand, but Luzern would need EU-grade compliance before managing a French captive.

- **2023 Finance Act.** Article 6 lets non-financial groups' reinsurance captives book a tax-free "resilience provision" [30]. Decree 2023-449 of 7 June 2023 sets the accounting rules [31].
- **Mechanics.** Yearly allocation is capped at 90% of technical profit on covered risks. The stock is capped at 10 times average minimum capital over three years [32][33].
- **Count.** The Trésor reported 22 reinsurance captives in mid-October 2025, against 10 at end-2022 and 4 at end-2019 [30]. Captive Review counted 23 at end-October 2025 [22]. AMRAE coverage cites 24 at end-2025 and about a dozen projects (single source) [35].
- **ACPR.** A French captive needs ACPR approval. The ACPR published an information guide on reinsurance captives on 12 November 2024 [34].
- **Solvency II.** The amending directive was published on 8 January 2025 and applies by 30 January 2027 [36]. "Small and non-complex" captives get lighter rules, including a two-yearly ORSA [37].
- **DORA.** It has applied to EU insurers and reinsurers since 17 January 2025. EIOPA flagged scope issues for small undertakings in November 2024 [38]. A manager running a captive's IT would face DORA vendor checks.
- **GDPR.** Claims and employee-benefit data are personal data, so EU processing or transfers need a GDPR basis.
- **AI Act.** Annex III point 5(c) makes AI for life and health insurance pricing of natural persons high-risk [39][40]. Corporate property and casualty captives fall outside that point; employee-benefit captives may not. The Commission issued draft classification guidelines in 2026 [39].

Net: tailwind on demand, headwind on compliance cost and local substance.

5. **FDJ (La Française des Jeux)** (plausible target) — reported to plan a French reinsurance captive [42].

**Recommended pitch:** "France went from 4 to 22 or more reinsurance captives in six years. Mid-sized French groups need a cheaper manager than the big brokers. Start with French brokers and AXA XL, then add an ACPR-ready service."

**Entry points:** ACPR (captive approval guide) [34]; Direction générale du Trésor (reform owner) [30]; AMRAE and its captive federation [35]; Business France and Choose France (from the radar).

- Only two founders are confirmed; the radar's other two names are unverified.
- Total raised varies by source: about $57M disclosed, $58.5M (radar), $59.8M (aggregator) [18].
- No named customer, captive count or premium volume is public.

- Luxembourg remains the EU reinsurance-captive hub, and Ireland and Malta compete [37].
- Does managing a French captive require ORIAS registration or a local entity? Verify with ACPR.
- AI Act exposure depends on whether Luzern prices health or life cover for individuals.
- The radar's rationale cites readiness 23, but its component reads 21; the total of 71 uses 21.
