## Product and business model

Antioch moves robot and drone testing from hardware into calibrated cloud simulation. It builds a simulation of the customer's hardware, sensors, software and models, calibrates it on real-world data, then runs thousands of scenarios in parallel [2].

- **Core product.** "Antioch Ark" packs the full hardware and software stack into one versioned artifact. It runs deterministically in simulation and on hardware, and plugs into CI/CD pipelines [6][8].
- **Agents.** The company calls itself an "agentic simulation platform" with "3D-native agents" for twin creation, environment generation and scenario testing (company job posting) [13].
- **Use cases.** Drones, mobile robots, industrial automation and smart security or fixed perception [11].
- **Positioning.** "The Cursor for physical AI" (TechCrunch, April 2026) [5].

Buyers are robotics, autonomy and perception engineering teams [3]. Onboarding is engineering-heavy: Simulation Engineers work "in tight loops with customers" for $180,000 to $230,000 a year (July 2026 posting, single source) [13].

Pricing and packaging are not disclosed. Differentiation rests on per-customer calibration and validation on held-out scenarios [3]. One commentary notes that no error rates or false-negative rates are published [12].

Antioch runs on Nebius GPU cloud. Nebius says the move from "a major cloud provider" cut total cost of ownership by 23%, sped cycles up to 50% and allowed 40% more parallel simulations (vendor case study, single source) [9]. No SOC 2, ISO 27001, on-premises or EU-region offer was found.

## Team

Antioch has five co-founders with autonomy, AI research and prior-exit experience. Harry Mellsop started the company with four others in May 2025 [5]. He worked on computer vision at Tesla [2]; pre-seed coverage calls him a former Tesla Autopilot engineer [6].

- **Alex Langshur and Michael Calvey** co-founded Transpose, which Chainalysis bought in May 2023 for an undisclosed sum [5][17]. Langshur is also listed as ex-head of product at Deep Grey Research (single source) [6].
- **Colton Swingle** (ex-Google DeepMind) and **Collin Schlager** (ex-Meta Reality Labs) complete the founding team [5].
- **Board.** Greylock general partner Saam Motamedi joined with the Series A [3].
- **Angels.** Shyam Sankar (Palantir CTO), Adrian Macneil (Foxglove CEO), Ian Andrews (NVIDIA executive, ex-Groq) [2][3].

Sources differ on Transpose. Robotics247 calls it a "security and intelligence platform" co-founded by Mellsop [4]; The Block calls it a blockchain data company [17]. No European staff were found; open roles are in New York [13].

## Regulatory fit for France and the EU

Regulation is mostly a customer tailwind and a compliance task for Antioch.

- **Machinery Regulation 2023/1230.** From 20 January 2027, machinery with AI safety components needs third-party conformity assessment [29]. An EU omnibus adds AI Act requirements [30]. Simulation could support test files; notified-body acceptance is unverified.
- **EU AI Act.** The tool itself is unlikely to be high-risk; customers' AI safety components are [29][30].
- **GDPR.** Most data is technical. Security-camera logs (Ring-type products) can show people, so a data processing agreement and a US transfer mechanism are needed.
- **NIS2.** It applies indirectly through in-scope French manufacturers and defence suppliers. No public SOC 2 or ISO 27001 is a headwind.
- **US export controls.** A BIS final rule (Federal Register, 14 August 2026) eased drone controls [31]. It raised the ECCN 9A012 endurance threshold from 30 minutes to three hours and amended 9D001, 9D002, 9D004 and 9E001 [32]. Military end-use limits remain [32]. Antioch's classification is not disclosed.
- **Sovereignty.** French defence buyers may require sovereign or on-premises deployment (data file). Antioch appears cloud-only.

**Recommended pitch.** French drone makers are moving to mass production for the DGA. Antioch lets them test each software change on calibrated digital twins before flight. Paris or Finland hosting via Nebius is worth exploring.

**Entry points.** Business France for landing; Aerospace Valley for the cluster; DGA/AID for defence pilots; France 2030 for co-funded projects. CIR and JEI apply only to a French R&D entity. Bpifrance already backs Genesis AI, a Paris rival [22].
