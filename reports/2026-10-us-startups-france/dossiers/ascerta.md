## Ascerta at a glance

Ascerta is a Bellevue, Washington software company that measures what enterprise AI costs, who uses it and which projects pay off. It raised an $18M Series A on 30 September 2026, taking total funding to $22.9M, and changed its name from Pay-i the same day [1][3]. For France it matters because Atos already uses it in its Sovereign Agentic Studios, a documented French reference [5][15].

## Product and business model

Ascerta sells what it calls "Enterprise AI Management": one system that links AI cost and usage to business outcomes [3][8]. Buyers are CIOs, CFOs and AI leaders [5][7].

**What changed with the rename.** Pay-i launched in May 2025 around AI cost management [3]. Customers then asked who used AI, what agents did and whether it justified more spend, so the scope widened [3][8].

**Products (company statements)** [5][10]:
- Atlas: AI value, adoption and ROI, from one workflow to the full portfolio.
- Forge: how engineering teams use coding agents, and the productivity gained.
- Convoy: for firms that provision their own AI capacity; adds use cases without disrupting production.

**How it works.** Ascerta measures cost below the token level, including hidden fees and negotiated discounts, by person, team and tool [7][10]. Instrumentation uses a Python SDK, a proxy with request headers and a REST ingest API [10] (single source). Listed integrations are OpenAI, Azure OpenAI/Microsoft Foundry, Databricks and n8n [10]. Series A coverage adds Microsoft Copilot, Bedrock AgentCore, Agentforce, GitHub Copilot, Claude Code and Codex [4][5].

**Pricing.** Not published; buyers contact sales [10] (single source).

**Differentiation.** Value, not token counts. Tepper: "The market is full of meaningless vanity metrics" [8].

**Certifications and hosting.** No SOC 2, ISO 27001, EU hosting or deployment-model statement surfaced. The only third-party security signal is Atos's screening of Scaler startups [16].

## Customers, traction and go-to-market

Atos and Wipro are the only named customers; insurers and others are unnamed [5].

- **Metrics (company claims).** Across customers: AI initiative ROI up 47%, agent launch time down 24%, wasted AI spend down 86% [1][2].
- **Insurer case.** A global insurer pooled agents onto shared hyperscaler capacity and saved about $3M in its first months [4] (single source; carrier not named).
- **Revenue.** At seed, Pay-i had no revenue and projected over $2M ARR by end 2026 from existing partnerships [11] (single source). Current revenue: not disclosed.
- **Channels.** Wipro as implementation partner [14]; the Atos Scaler ecosystem [15][16].

**The Atos relationship.**
- *What it is.* Atos Group launched the Atos Sovereign Agentic Studios (SAS) on 12 March 2026 [15]. It moves agentic AI from pilot to production "under full sovereign control", across four flagship geographies and ten Global Delivery Centers [15][17].
- *Scaler cohort.* Atos's accelerator picked KYP.ai, Ema, Pay-i, Klarity, Poolside and Noma Security, screened on technology, security and compliance [15][16].
- *Pay-i's role.* Atos describes it as "real-time visibility into the cost, performance, and business value of every agent in production" [16]. Tepper says the tie-up joins sovereign infrastructure, security and financial governance in one integrated offering [16].
- *Endorsement.* Atos Group CTO Florin Rotar: SAS requires "measurable business value, not just technical capability"; "Ascerta has been instrumental in delivering on that promise" [5][7].
- *What Scaler gives.* At its 2020 launch: business-development experts, coaching, data hosting and Atos Studio, for about 15 startups a year [18]. 2026 terms are not documented.
- *Joint offer.* None found beyond Tepper's wording [16].

## Regulatory fit for France and the EU

EU rules are mostly a tailwind for a spend-and-usage governance tool, but per-employee tracking creates GDPR duties.

- **GDPR.** Tracking AI use "by person" [10] processes employee personal data. Ascerta needs a DPA, a lawful basis, pseudonymised reports and a US transfer mechanism. Staff-representative consultation may apply; verify with counsel.
- **EU AI Act.** Article 12 makes providers of high-risk systems build automatic logging. Article 26 makes deployers keep those logs for at least six months [28]. A May 2026 Digital Omnibus deal defers high-risk duties to 2 December 2027 (Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I) [29]. Final adoption was not verified. Per-agent records could support deployer logging.
- **DORA.** In force since 17 January 2025 for banks, insurers and investment firms [30]. They must register ICT third-party providers, including software vendors [30]. Ascerta would need DORA-ready contract terms.
- **Sovereignty.** Atos sells control over where data and models run [15]; BNP Paribas hosts LLMs in its own data centres [35]. Ascerta needs an EU-hosted or self-hosted option.
- **Certifications.** None found. Rivals advertise SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 [22][23].
- **CSRD.** The radar's CSRD angle is unsupported: no energy feature was found.

- Crowded market: CloudZero and Finout market AI ROI [19][20]; Microsoft and OpenAI ship native tools [24][34].
- No certification, EU hosting or GDPR documentation found.
- Revenue unknown; the $2M ARR target for 2026 is unconfirmed.
- Is Atos a paid production user or a programme pilot? Unknown.
- The AI Act delay's final legal status needs checking.
